Two hundred dollars a month can quietly become almost seven hundred thousand. Most people think getting rich needs a big salary or one lucky investment. But the most powerful force in personal finance is boring. It's time. Imagine investing two hundred dollars every month from age twenty-five to sixty-five. That's ninety-six thousand dollars of your own money. At an eight percent average yearly return, it could grow to about six hundred ninety-eight thousand. More than seven times what you put in. Now imagine starting ten years later, at thirty-five. Same two hundred a month. You'd end with about two hundred ninety-eight thousand. Less than half. Those ten years cost you roughly four hundred thousand dollars. This is why compound growth feels slow at first and unstoppable later. At eight percent, money doubles about every nine years. It's called the rule of seventy-two: divide seventy-two by your yearly return, and you get the years it takes to double. Of course, returns are never guaranteed, and markets never rise in a straight line. But the math rewards whoever starts first, not whoever earns the most. So set up an automatic transfer today, even a small one. Because two hundred dollars a month isn't small. It's seven hundred thousand that hasn't happened yet.
