Every purchase costs more than its price. Most people ask only one question before they buy: can I afford it? But there's a second price that never shows up on the receipt. Economists call it opportunity cost: what that money could have become instead. Imagine spending one thousand dollars on something you'll forget in a month. Invested at an eight percent yearly return, that same thousand could grow to about ten thousand dollars in thirty years. So the real price wasn't one thousand. It was ten. Now think about the small things that repeat. A hundred and fifty dollars a month on subscriptions, takeout and apps you barely use. Invested instead for thirty years, that's roughly two hundred twenty-three thousand dollars. This is why wealthy people don't just read the price tag. They read the price tag times time. Of course, you shouldn't stop enjoying your life, and not every dollar has to be invested. But before your next purchase, try this: multiply the price by ten. If it's still worth it, buy it without guilt. Because every purchase costs more than its price. The only question is whether it's worth what it could have become.
