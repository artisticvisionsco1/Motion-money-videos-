Being short on money doesn't just empty your wallet. It fills your mind. Most people think bad money choices come from bad character. But in twenty thirteen, researchers led by Anandi Mani and Sendhil Mullainathan studied sugarcane farmers in India, who get paid once a year, at harvest. Before the harvest, when money was tight, the same farmers scored worse on thinking tests than after it. The drop was similar to losing about nine or ten IQ points. Same people. Only their bank balance changed. Money worries take up mental space. Of course, the fix isn't to try harder. It's to build a small cushion. Start with one week of expenses, set aside automatically. Because being short on money doesn't just empty your wallet. It fills your mind.
