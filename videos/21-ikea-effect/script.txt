You'll pay more for something you built yourself. Most people think we value things for how good they are. But in a study published in twenty twelve, researchers Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon and Dan Ariely had people assemble plain IKEA storage boxes. Then they asked how much they would pay for them. The builders offered about sixty-three percent more than people who got the same box already assembled. They called it the IKEA effect. Effort makes things feel valuable. That's why a business you built, or a stock you researched, can be hard to judge honestly. Of course, pride in your work is a good thing. But before a big money decision, ask a friend who didn't build it. Because you'll pay more for something you built yourself, even when it isn't worth more.
