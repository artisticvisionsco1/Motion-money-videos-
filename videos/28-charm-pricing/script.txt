A higher price can make you buy more. Most people think lower prices always sell better. But in two thousand three, professors Eric Anderson and Duncan Simester tested prices in a women's clothing catalog. The same dress was listed at thirty-four, thirty-nine and forty-four dollars. The thirty-nine dollar version sold the most. More than at thirty-four, even though it cost five dollars more. Prices ending in nine feel like a deal, and our brain reads the first digit and moves on. Of course, sometimes the deal is real. But next time you see a price ending in nine, round it up in your head. Thirty-nine is forty. Nine ninety-nine is ten. Because a higher price can make you buy more, when it ends in nine.
