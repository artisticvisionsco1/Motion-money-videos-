You value things more the moment they become yours. Most people believe a price is just a price. But in nineteen ninety, Daniel Kahneman, Jack Knetsch and Richard Thaler handed coffee mugs to half the students in a room, and let them trade. The students who got a mug wanted about seven dollars to sell it. The students without one would only pay about three. Same mug. Same room. Owning it for a few minutes more than doubled its value in their minds. This is called the endowment effect. It's why people overprice their used cars and their homes. It's why free trials work so well: once something feels like yours, giving it back feels like a loss. And it's why closets slowly fill with things you never use but can't let go of. Of course, some things really are worth more to you, and memories are real value. But before you keep, buy or sell something, ask: if I didn't own this, would I pay this price for it today? Because you value things more the moment they become yours, and sellers know it.
