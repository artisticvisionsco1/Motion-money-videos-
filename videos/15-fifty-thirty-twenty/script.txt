Most budgets fail because they're too complicated. Most people track every coffee for a week, then give up. But there's a simpler rule, popularized by Elizabeth Warren and her daughter in their book All Your Worth. Fifty percent of your after-tax income goes to needs, like rent, food and bills. Thirty percent goes to wants. And twenty percent goes to savings and paying off debt. On a four thousand dollar monthly income, that's two thousand for needs, twelve hundred for wants, and eight hundred for your future. Of course, the exact split can change with your life. But the idea stays the same: decide where your money goes before it leaves. Because most budgets fail because they're too complicated. This one fits on a sticky note.
