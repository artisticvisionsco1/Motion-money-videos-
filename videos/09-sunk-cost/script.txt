The money you already lost is controlling your next decision. Most people believe they should finish what they paid for. But for years, Britain and France kept pouring money into the Concorde jet, long after it was clear it would never pay for itself. Economists still call this the Concorde fallacy. It's the same reason people sit through a bad movie because they bought the ticket, or keep repairing a car that keeps breaking. The money is gone either way. Only your next choice is still yours. Of course, sometimes sticking with something is the right call. So ask one question: if I were starting today, would I choose this again? Because the money you already lost can't come back. Your next decision still can.
