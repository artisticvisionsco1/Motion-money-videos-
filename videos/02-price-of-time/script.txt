Your salary is the price of your time, not the value of it. Most people believe their paycheck shows how much their work is really worth. But a salary is simply the amount a company agreed to pay so that you stay. Imagine earning twenty-five dollars an hour. Work forty hours a week for a full year, and that's about fifty-two thousand dollars. Now imagine your work helps the company bring in three times that amount. Your value didn't change. Only the price of your time did. This is why two people can work equally hard and still earn completely different amounts. One is paid for hours. The other is paid for results. And hours have a ceiling. There are only about two thousand working hours in a year, and you can never sell the same hour twice. Of course, a steady salary isn't a bad thing. It pays the bills, and it buys you time to learn. But the moment you see the difference between your price and your value, you start asking a different question. Not how many hours can I sell, but what result can I create. Because your salary is the price of your time. Your value is what you build with it.
