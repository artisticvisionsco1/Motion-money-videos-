One new purchase can make everything else you own look old. In seventeen sixty-nine, the French philosopher Denis Diderot was given a beautiful new scarlet robe. Suddenly, his old furniture looked shabby next to it. So he replaced his chair, then his desk, then the tapestry on the wall, until he was surrounded by new things and much poorer. He wrote about it in an essay called Regrets on Parting with My Old Dressing Gown. Today we call it the Diderot effect. A new phone needs a new case. A new sofa needs a new rug. Of course, upgrading isn't always wrong. But before you buy something new, ask what else it will make you want. Because one new purchase can make everything else look old, and that's when the real spending starts.
