The easiest way to save more is to promise it later. Most people know they should save more, but today's money always feels too tight. In two thousand four, economists Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi tested a simple idea called Save More Tomorrow. Workers didn't raise their savings today. Instead, they agreed that part of every future raise would go to savings automatically. The results were huge. In one company, savings rates rose from about three and a half percent to almost fourteen percent in under four years. Nobody felt poorer, because their paycheck never went down. They simply never got used to spending the extra. This is why willpower is the wrong tool. Your future self is a much easier person to commit. Of course, it only works if the plan is automatic, and you don't cancel it when the raise arrives. So today, decide what share of your next raise you'll save, and set it up before the money ever reaches you. Because the easiest way to save more is to promise it later, and let the system keep that promise for you.
