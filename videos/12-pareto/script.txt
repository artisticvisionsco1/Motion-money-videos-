Twenty percent of your effort probably creates eighty percent of your results. In the eighteen nineties, the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto noticed that about twenty percent of the people in Italy owned about eighty percent of the land. Later, people found the same uneven pattern almost everywhere. A small share of customers brings most of a company's revenue. A few habits drive most of your health. A handful of tasks create most of your income. Most people believe every hour of work is worth about the same. But it rarely is. Imagine working fifty hours a week. If ten of those hours produce most of the value, the other forty are mostly busy work. Double those ten hours, and your results could grow far more than if you simply worked longer. This is why working harder isn't always the answer. Working on the right twenty percent usually is. Of course, eighty twenty isn't an exact law, and real numbers are rarely that neat. But here's a simple start: list everything you did last week, and circle the few things that actually made money. Because twenty percent of your effort creates eighty percent of your results. The question is whether you know which twenty.
