The option you never choose decides most of your money. Most people believe they make their own retirement decisions. But in two thousand one, economists Brigitte Madrian and Dennis Shea studied a company that changed just one thing. New workers were enrolled in the retirement plan automatically, unless they opted out. Participation jumped from about thirty-seven percent to eighty-six percent. Same people. Same pay. Just a different default. We tend to stick with whatever is already set. Of course, defaults can work against you too, like subscriptions that quietly renew. So check every default in your money life, and set the good ones on purpose. Because the option you never choose decides most of your money.
