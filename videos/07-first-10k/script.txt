The first ten thousand dollars is harder than the next hundred thousand. Most people believe getting rich is a straight line, where every dollar takes the same effort as the one before it. But money doesn't grow in a straight line. Imagine saving three hundred dollars a month. It would take you almost three years just to reach ten thousand. And at that point, it feels painfully slow. Invested at eight percent, that ten thousand earns you around eight hundred dollars a year. Barely noticeable. But at a hundred thousand, the same eight percent earns eight thousand a year, without you lifting a finger. Your money starts doing part of the work. And it isn't only about interest. The first ten thousand also teaches you how to earn, how to save, and how to say no. Those habits make every next step easier. This is why most people quit early. They judge the whole journey by its slowest part. Of course, investing always carries risk, and returns are never guaranteed. But the hardest part of building wealth usually isn't the middle. It's the beginning. Because the first ten thousand isn't just money. It's proof that you can do it, and that proof is what makes the next hundred thousand possible.
