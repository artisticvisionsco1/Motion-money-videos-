The thing you think will make you happy forever usually fades in months. Most people believe more money, a bigger house or a new car will lift their mood for good. But in nineteen seventy-eight, psychologists Philip Brickman, Dan Coates and Ronnie Janoff-Bulman interviewed people who had won big lottery prizes. Months after their win, the winners were not much happier than ordinary people. And they enjoyed everyday pleasures, like talking with a friend, a little less. We adapt to almost everything. Of course, money still matters when it covers real needs. But before a big purchase, ask how you will feel about it after six months, not six minutes. Because the thing you think will make you happy forever usually fades in months.
