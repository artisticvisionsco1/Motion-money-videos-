A raise rarely makes people richer. Most people believe their money problems will disappear at the next income level. But when income rises, spending quietly rises with it. A better car. A bigger apartment. Nicer dinners, because now you can afford them. Economists call this lifestyle creep. Psychologists call the feeling behind it the hedonic treadmill: the joy of every upgrade fades, so you need the next one. Imagine getting a ten thousand dollar raise and spending all of it. You earn more, yet you are exactly as far from freedom as before. Of course, enjoying some of it is fine. So next time your pay goes up, save at least half of the raise before you ever see it. Because a raise doesn't make you richer. What you keep from it does.
