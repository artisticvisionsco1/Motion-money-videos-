There's a simple trick that tells you how fast money doubles. Most people guess, and they guess badly. But in fourteen ninety-four, the Italian mathematician Luca Pacioli wrote down a shortcut we still use today: the rule of seventy-two. Divide seventy-two by the yearly rate, and you get the years it takes to double. At eight percent, your money doubles in about nine years. At two percent, it takes thirty-six. And a credit card at twenty-four percent can double your debt in just three. Of course, it's an estimate, not an exact answer. But before you save, invest or borrow, run the numbers: seventy-two divided by the rate. Because there's a simple trick that tells you how fast money doubles, and it works on debt too.
