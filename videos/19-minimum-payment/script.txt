Paying the minimum on a credit card can almost triple what you owe. Most people think that as long as they pay something every month, they're fine. But imagine a five thousand dollar balance at twenty-two percent interest. If you pay one hundred dollars a month, it takes more than eleven years to clear it, and you pay almost eight thousand seven hundred dollars in interest alone. That's thirteen thousand seven hundred dollars for a five thousand dollar debt. Raise the payment to two hundred fifty dollars, and the same debt is gone in just over two years, with about thirteen hundred dollars in interest. The difference isn't your income. It's the speed. This is why credit card interest feels invisible: the monthly payment looks small, while the total quietly grows. Of course, sometimes the minimum is all you can manage, and that's still better than missing a payment. But whenever you can, pay more than the minimum, and attack the highest interest debt first. Because paying the minimum can almost triple what you owe, and the bank is happy to wait.
