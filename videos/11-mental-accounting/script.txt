Your tax refund isn't free money. It's your own money, returned late. Most people treat a refund like a bonus, a little reward for doing their taxes. But economist Richard Thaler, who later won a Nobel Prize, showed that we sort money into mental accounts. Salary goes in one box. Gifts, refunds and windfalls go in another, and that box feels much easier to spend. Imagine having two hundred fifty dollars too much withheld from every monthly paycheck. That's three thousand dollars a year you lent to the government at zero percent interest. Then it comes back as one big check, it feels like a gift, and it disappears on things you would never have bought with your salary. This is why the same dollar can feel cheap or precious depending on where it came from. Of course, a refund isn't bad, and for some people it works as forced saving. But try this: before you spend any windfall, imagine it arrived in your regular paycheck. Would you still spend it the same way? Because your tax refund isn't free money. It only feels free because you got it late.
