Your savings can lose value while the number stays the same. Most people think money in the bank is perfectly safe. But if prices rise three percent a year, one hundred dollars today will buy only about seventy-four dollars worth of things in ten years. In twenty years, about fifty-five. The bill in your wallet doesn't change. What it can buy quietly shrinks. This is why inflation is often called a hidden tax. Nobody sends you a bill, but every year your money does a little less. Of course, you still need cash for emergencies. But money you won't touch for years should grow faster than prices, not sit still. Because your savings can lose value while the number stays the same, and that's exactly what makes it so easy to miss.
