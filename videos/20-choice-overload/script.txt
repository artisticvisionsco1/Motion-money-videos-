More choice can make you buy nothing at all. In two thousand, psychologists Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper set up a jam tasting stand in a grocery store. Some days it offered twenty-four flavors. Other days, only six. The big display attracted more people, but only three percent of them bought a jar. With six flavors, thirty percent did. Ten times more sales, with fewer options. Too many choices make every decision feel risky, so we delay it. Of course, choice itself isn't bad. But when a money decision feels overwhelming, cut it down to three options, then pick one. Because more choice can make you buy nothing at all, and doing nothing is a choice too.
